# P1: the adapter

P1 turns Quantum-Film's records of its runs, and IBM's and Moth's own, into QRO-Crates, and holds a crate's
commitments, decodes and calibration links to the bytes it carries. It is two halves.
- **The checker's half (P1.1):** steps 4, 5 and 8, and the readers of the three raw forms.
- **The producer's half (P1.2):** the records' readers, the two generators that step 9 runs, the adapter, the seven
  controls and the twin they are planted in, and the tests that read the real crates.

This page is the producer's half, and states what P1.1's steps rest on where the two meet. The design is P1's
(ledger, 2026-10-15T22:53:52Z), with the lead's decisions on it. Every claim here is held by a test or says it is not.

## The two crates

records of 2026-10-14. Nothing of either is committed. Moth's data never enters the repository, and IBM's calibration
snapshots are not licensed to; the adapter refuses an `--out` inside the repository.

## The generators

A generator, called with the crate's directory, returns the metadata of a crate from its files and **nothing else**: never
`ro-crate-metadata.json`, so what the crate carries cannot move what is regenerated from its records. It classifies every file by
its path into one of the adapter's layouts, a closed list; parses the records; holds them to each other; and builds the
entities. The header terms are constants, which step 9 normalises away and the adapter replaces.

**No CHECKED value is copied from a record.** Each is made by the function the step that checks it uses:

| term | made by |
|---|---|
| `qro:commitment` | `p1_commit.commitment_of`, from the record's fourteen inputs |
| `qro:valuesDigest`, `qro:stamp`, `qro:newestValue` | `p1_calibration.claims`, from the snapshot's file |
| `qro:calibrationLink`, `qro:calibration` | `p1_calibration.link_for`, from the raw result's first shot and the snapshots |
| `qro:decodeRule`, `qro:bitOrder` | `p1_decode.RULES` |
| `qro:knownAnswer` | the Pub of role `known-answer` of the Job's bundle, found by the bundle digest |
| `qro:shots`, `startTime`, `endTime` | `p1_forms`, from the raw result's bytes |

The design's T1 to T3 hold this, in `tests/p1/test_p1_generate.py`: the output is byte-identical whatever the metadata file holds
(absent, garbage, empty, another crate's); each producing function patched to return a sentinel puts it in the output when the
guards are switched off (every guard goes through `_hold`, the one place a test switches them off); and a record's own claim
changed raises.

### The guards

A build from records that disagree fails loudly, as a `GuardError` naming the guard, the place and the reason; on a crate that
carries such records, step 9 refuses `metadata-generated`. Each guard has a planted fault that trips it and no other first
(`tests/p1/test_p1_generate.py`, 114 planted faults; G11's in `test_p1_atlas_generate.py`, 31 planted faults; `test_p1_page.py`
holds every count of tests or plants this page states to the counted one). Where a guard's comparisons imply one another
in a planted crate (G2 and G3 hold the manifest's, the line's and the record's commitment to one value before G4 looks, so a
planted crate cannot tell which of G4's four comparisons fired), the comparisons are tried one by one on the guard's own function,
and so is the bundle's one known-answer job and Pub (G2).

| | the guard |
|---|---|
| G1 | each file a manifest names, where present, hashes as the manifest says; no bundle file is one it does not name; the source circuit the manifest names is in the crate; the manifest's `options_sha256` is the digest of its `options` |
| G2 | a job's line holds its manifest's bundle digest, its commitments in PUB order, stock, kind, route, backend and program; its marker names its job and bundle; a bundle's jobs have exactly one known-answer job |
| G3 | a job has at most ten PUBs; each device-run record's digest is the SHA-256 of its body, its source and role agree with the manifest entry and the line; every PUB has one record |
| G4 | the commitment recomputed from the record's inputs is the manifest's, the line's, the record's and IBM's echo |
| G5 | the rule is one IBM's counts can read (an IBM manifest naming Atlas's is a refusal, not a crash); the raw result holds a PUB for each commitment of the line, with the manifest's shots, and the decode's counts are the record's, exactly |
| G6 | the metrics wrapper names the job and device and holds the line's submitted time as IBM's created time; the span lies in [running, finished] |
| G7 | the read's job record: its flags hold, its first shot is the span's start and its running time the metrics'; the snapshots IBM's history returned, before the first shot and before running, are carried whatever the job's span, and the first is valid for the first shot |
| G8 | the read's result record holds the raw result's digest and size, its path, and says IBM's copy is the same bytes |
| G9 | each of the read's two MANIFESTs is carried wherever its directory holds files, and lists each file of that directory, as it hashes; every snapshot they list of a device the crate carries jobs of is carried. So a snapshot of the read dropped from the crate, or dropped with its MANIFEST, cannot move a link verdict, unless the read's MANIFEST is edited to match, which is limit 1's producer: the twin's 12:21:18 snapshot taken out with its MANIFEST entry, and the metadata regenerated, is accepted with sheet-02's job moved from `history-in-gap` to `history-at-first-shot` (`test_p1_controls.py` holds it). On the real crate the same edit is refused, as a job's read record names that snapshot (G7; MEASURED by P1.2's third verifier). (MEASURED: in the read of 2026-10-14 each directory holding files, its seven snapshots' among them, holds its MANIFEST, and every crate the adapter builds carries both.) Quantum-Film's own five snapshots lie in directories no MANIFEST lists, as its records keep none: limit 4 |
| G10 | each snapshot is in the closed form, of its device, and stamped as its file is named |
| G11 | (Atlas) every commitments line recomputes under version 2, names Atlas's decode and a commit, whether or not its job's files are in the crate, and a line whose job has no files in the crate is refused (MEASURED: the real records hold none, 1 line and 1 set of files on 2026-09-25 and 5 and 5 on 2026-09-26); each job's submit, status, result and score files agree with its line, the circuit it names and its shots |
| G12 | every file classifies |

**No @id is written twice.** The entities of a crate are collected in one place, and a second write of an @id is a raise of the
path's guard, G2 on the IBM path and G11 on Atlas's, so that two records that make the same @id cannot overwrite one another and
leave a file no entity reads (an Atlas vector whose job is a law job's did). An entity every record that needs it makes the same, a
program or a transpiler, is written once and said again as no write.

Together they cover what the steps concede (below) for the fields the generator derives a value from, or holds to another record,
which is most of what a record says: a changed one is a changed value or a raise, and so step 9, noticing. A field it derives nothing
from and holds to nothing is not covered; limit 21 names the classes the checker holds by their digests alone.

## The adapter

```
python -m qro.p1_adapter ibm|atlas --moth DIR --out DIR [--records DIR] [--slice]
```

It copies the records the crate carries, byte for byte, to their paths; asks P2's module for the files of the question, if it is
present; generates the metadata; sets the header (the date, the writer, the time); and writes it with `qro.canonical.write`, which
holds it to the strict reader. A build that fails removes what it made, and nothing else (limit 22). The checkout is only read: the
adapter's git calls take no optional lock, so not even its index is refreshed. git's output, its stdout and its stderr, is read as
bytes and decoded as UTF-8, git's own spelling of a path on every host, so that no name in the checkout, under any `core.quotePath`,
and no error of git's decides the exit or adds a line: a git that fails is a checkout the adapter will not use, misuse, and the first
line it said, which may name a path, is shown in the line. git is asked without replace objects, so a ref of the checkout's own
(`git replace`) cannot make another commit's tree show as HEAD's.

It refuses, with exit 2 and one line: a checkout that is not moth-quantum clean at 3a73823 (`git status` finds a change or an
untracked file, `--untracked-files=all` whatever the checkout's own `status.showUntrackedFiles` says; or `git ls-files -v` marks a file
assume-unchanged or skip-worktree, which `git status` cannot see); a file the build would take from the checkout that is not
3a73823's own, whatever the checkout's own settings say of what git shows (an ignore rule in `.git/info/exclude`, a `.gitignore` or
a `core.excludesFile`; an attribute or a filter, a line-ending conversion among them): its path is not a regular file of HEAD's tree
(`git ls-tree -r -z --full-tree`, mode 100644 or 100755; a link, mode 120000, is not, whatever bytes are checked out in its place),
or the bytes the build copies, from the one read it copies, are not that blob's by git's own hash of a blob (SHA-1 for a
40-character id, SHA-256 for a 64-character one), the path in the checkout named in the line and which it is, and nothing left (a
slice is not a checkout and is not asked); a `--moth` or a
`--records` the host will not let the adapter look at, as it asks what the input is or holds and lists its directories, or starts
git in the checkout (a share whose access is denied, a directory the host will not list, which `os.walk` alone would pass over as
empty), the input named in the line and the place in it the host refused; an `--out` that is not empty, that lies in this
repository or in the checkout by any spelling (through a link, a `.` or `..`, another case, or the extended-length prefix `\\?\` or
`//?/`, which `os.path.realpath` keeps and the guard drops on both sides; limit 23 states what it does not see), that names no
directory an entry can be made in (a network, extended-length or device spelling with no share or no name, such as `\\`,
`\\server`, `\\?\`, `\\.\` or `\\?\UNC\server`, onto which pathlib joins an entry as a server, a share or a device and not in
it, told before the host is asked anything; or a root that is not there, a share the host cannot reach or a drive that is not),
that the host cannot look at (a directory it cannot list), or that it cannot make, or cannot make the first entry under (a file
in its way, a name the host will not take, a last component ending in a space, which Windows makes without the space and then
finds nothing under, a path of 32,768 characters or more, which Windows refuses with a ValueError and no command line can carry),
the out named in the line; and a link where the plan takes a file or walks a directory of the records (the IBM records', the
read's, an Atlas day's files, the licence, the source circuit, the vector).
Exit 1 and one `REFUSED` line is the records refused: records that disagree (`REFUSED metadata-generated`, the guard and the place in
the detail), a job line of a slice that is no line (G2, with the file named), a decode rule IBM's counts cannot read (G5), a record
holding a string UTF-8 cannot encode (a lone surrogate, written as an escape, at any depth the strict reader admits: the guard that
reads the record, with the record and its field named, a key's as the object that holds it), a record nested deeper than the
strict reader reads, and text the metadata writer will not write, such as a program name or a Qiskit version with a space in it
(`REFUSED spelling at @graph[N] @id`, the writer's own refusal). Exit 3 is the adapter itself failing: no git on the PATH; a record
the host will not let the adapter read once the build is under way (one it copies, or a job line a slice is read for); the host
refusing an entry after the first under `--out` (a full disk, a name it takes at the first entry and refuses later), another's
file where the build writes (which is never overwritten), each with the world restored; and a verdict that cannot be written.
`BUILT` counts the files and bytes the build wrote. The line is printable ASCII, written to the descriptor itself, as
`python -m qro check` writes its own.

`--slice` takes a directory laid out as moth-quantum's that holds a part of its records (the twin) and skips the git check and
HEAD's tree: a slice is not a checkout and is not asked. `--slice` given twice is misuse, exit 2, as every option given twice is.
**A slice is taken as it is.** The adapter does not check what it builds; the checker does. A slice that lacks a file a carried
job's commitment binds (the twin without `coherence-xx.qasm`, the circuit the morning coherence job's commitment binds) builds, exit
0, a crate the checker refuses, `commitment` (`tests/p1/test_p1_adapter.py` pins it). A checkout cannot lose a file without `git
status` or `git ls-files -v` saying so, but for one whose own configuration makes git say what is not so: under a `core.fsmonitor`
hook that reports no change, a file deleted from the checkout is seen by neither, and the build takes the checkout without that
file, since it holds each file it takes to HEAD's tree and not HEAD's tree to the files it takes (P1.2's seventh verifier, F1,
MEASURED: one of Quantum-Film's five snapshots deleted, the IBM crate built of 1,072 files (as then built) and accepted, as limit 4 says of a crate
without one of them). A slice takes of the read only the records of the jobs it carries and every snapshot of their devices. An IBM slice whose `--moth`
holds none of the records makes a crate that mentions no action, which the profile does not spell: the writer refuses it, exit 1,
`REFUSED spelling at ./ mentions: a list of one value or more`, a true refusal in words that name the profile's rule, not what is
missing; an Atlas one is refused by G11, which names the missing vector (both MEASURED).

## The seam with P2

P2 writes `qro.p2_measures` with two functions, and P1's generators call them if the module is present, found as the checker finds
a parcel's (in `sys.modules`, or by `importlib.util.find_spec`), and never otherwise. The module present without both is a `seam` raise.
- `question_files(crate_dir) -> {path relative to question/: bytes}`: called by the **adapter**, after the records are copied and before
  the metadata is generated; each name must be a path in the crate's own spelling; the files are written under `question/`. The answer
  must be a dict.
- `contribute(crate_dir, entities) -> (question_terms, new_entities)`: called by the **generator**, with a `Path` and a deep copy of
  `{@id: entity}` for every entity P1 wrote but the descriptor, the root and the two Profile entities. `question_terms` (a dict) join
  `#question`; a term P1 writes there is a `seam` raise. `new_entities` (a list, or a dict by @id) join the graph; an @id P1 holds is a
  `seam` raise. The root's `mentions` is rebuilt from every action. P2's files under `question/` are Files of the root's licence,
  listed by a Dataset `#question-files`.

**When P2 has no law for a crate** (the lead's ruling, 2026-11-08). `question_files` answers `{}` for a crate that holds no source
circuit P2 knows, so the adapter writes nothing under `question/`. Both generators call `contribute` **only for a crate that holds
files under `question/`**, so P2's `contribute`, which raises for a crate with no law, is never asked about one. Such a crate carries
none of P2's terms or entities: `#question` holds P1's five terms, there is no `#question-files`, no `#measuring`, no Measurement,
Contrast or Hold. Steps 6 and 7 accept it, as every step accepts a crate that carries none of its terms (limit 19), and step 9's
generator makes the same answer, so a crate built either way regenerates as built. The cost, stated: a crate of another question, or of
a source circuit whose bytes moved, is built and accepted with no measures, and claims none; and so is a crate built without
P2's module, or one whose `question/` files and P2's entities are taken out together and its metadata regenerated (below). Refusing it instead would make P1's
building of any other question depend on P2's knowing it. Held in `tests/p1/test_p1_merged.py` (a synthetic Atlas slice, whose circuit
is none P2 knows, built as a process with P2's own module, accepted by the whole checker, regenerated as built) and, with a stand-in,
in `tests/p1/test_p1_seam.py` (`contribute` never called without a file under `question/`, on both paths).

**When P2 raises.** A raise of `question_files` or `contribute` during a build, whatever its class, is the records refused by the seam:
a `GuardError` `seam` at `qro.p2_measures` whose detail is `<function> raised <class>: <P2's message>`, so exit 1 and one line
`REFUSED metadata-generated at qro.p2_measures: seam: question_files raised ValueError: ...`, with the world restored; never exit 3.
The two exceptions are the host's: an `OSError` (a `PermissionError` and a `FileNotFoundError` among them) and a `MemoryError` pass as
they are and keep the adapter's rules, exit 3 with the world restored. A function that answers what is no dict (`question_files`), no
pair of a dict and a list or dict (`contribute`) is a `seam` raise too. At check time a generator's raise is refused by step 9's name,
`metadata-generated`, as it is for any parcel's generator, and a `MemoryError` is the checker failing. Held as a process, with a
stand-in module that raises in each function (`tests/p1/test_p1_seam.py`, with `tests/p1/p1_standin.py`).

**One tree per test.** A test that means P1 alone pins P2's module absent (`pin_p2`) for its build and its generator both, and takes
the twin built with it absent (`twin_copy(alone=True)`, `Sandbox(alone=True)`, `plants_p1.pristine_twin_alone`); a test that means the
tree as merged pins nothing and gets P2's module for both (`tests/p1/test_p1_merged.py`, `p1-crates`, the controls, `twin_copy()`).
None builds with one and generates with the other. The twin is 73 files with P2's module absent, 74 with it (P2's
`question/law.json`).

A crate built before P2 lands holds no file under `question/`, so once P2 is present the generator calls no `contribute` for it:
it regenerates as built and is accepted, with none of P2's measures (MEASURED by P1.2's eighth verifier: the real IBM crate
built with P2 absent, `8ec4dc39…`, accepted by the checker with P2 present). So the checker cannot tell a crate P2 has no law
for from one built without P2, or stripped of P2's files and entities; such a crate claims no measures. Having the generator
ask `question_files` whether P2 has a law for the crate would close it, and is left for a later piece. A P2 module that
cannot be imported is no `seam` raise: the adapter fails, exit 3, as for any module of the tree it cannot load, and at
check time the generator's failure is refused `metadata-generated` (P1.2's eighth verifier, note 2).

## What each step concedes, and what covers it

These are stated limits. Each is held by a test as a limit (accepted), not left as a gap.

1. **A producer who holds every byte can reseal a crate, and the crate-only checker accepts it** (`qpy-only`,
   `shot-moved-resealed`, a fabricated run with fresh identities). Only the QPY stage and the owner's re-fetch refuse. Its scale
   (MEASURED by P1.2's second verifier, two leaves of each class of the twin altered and deleted, every dependent digest resealed
   to a fixpoint): 1,042 of 2,279 leaf edits are accepted, among them the gate lists, a bundle manifest's entries (28 of the real
   crate's 93 manifest jobs never ran, so their entries are carried), the read's copies of IBM's fields, raw-result fields no
   reader uses, snapshots' measurement dates, and limit 21's classes.
2. **IBM's echo shows the bytes carry the commitment, not that the bytes are IBM's.** That is the re-fetch's.
3. **Atlas's commitment is `none`:** nothing evidences that it came before the result. The commitment lines are plain files.
4. **A `history-*` link is a necessary condition, not proof the snapshot was in force.** The gap rule sees only the snapshots the
   crate carries. The generator refuses a crate that lacks a snapshot the read's records name or its MANIFESTs list, of a device the
   crate carries, or that holds files of the read's directory without that directory's MANIFEST (G7, G9; MEASURED: the honest crate
   leaves out of the read only the four files of the dry-run probes, which no MANIFEST lists, and carries both MANIFESTs and all
   seven of its snapshots). The five snapshots of Quantum-Film's own are named by no record: taken out
   of the real crate, each regenerates and is accepted, and no link moves, since each predates the runs (MEASURED; a test in
   `p1-crates` holds one).
5. **The values-digest keeps list order:** a provider that re-ordered a list between fetches of one calibration would show two
   digests (a missed EQUAL, never a false one).
6. **A known answer holds the order only as far as its pattern tells orders apart.** IBM: a set that is its own mirror is
   refused. Atlas: one frozen vector, 128 Z-basis positions within |z| <= 4; X positions are untested and Y positions cannot tell
   qubits apart.
7. **`read-before-run`, `read-after-run` and `provider-linked` are refused `step-unbuilt`.**
8. **Spans are zone-less and read as UTC;** no clock offset is measured.
9. **No step reads Quantum-Film's decoded records or verdicts.** The generator checks the records against the re-decode; no gate
   reads the verdict file (`an honest crate with a verdict file that lies is accepted`). Limit 21 lists what else is carried unread.
10. **A PUB index is one decimal digit of an id:** ten PUBs (0 to 9) are accepted; an eleventh needs a new rule.
11. **A Pub weakened to `none` binds nothing.** The bit order a known answer is judged by rests on whatever binds the known-answer
    Pub's record, so with that Pub alone at `none`, a mirrored circuit and `lsb-first` Decodes pass steps 1 to 8. A Job weakened to
    `unknown` or `unexposed` passes step 8 where its raw result carries no span. Dropping or relabelling an Atlas law Pub's role
    skips step 5's layout accounting. **Step 9 is the cover for all of these.**
12. **The twin has no law job and no Atlas data;** the controls at the scale of a law job are in `p1-crates`.
13. **Real-crate plants use hard links;** the toolkit unlinks before it writes, and a test checks the source.
14. **In-crate paths are at most 77 characters** (MEASURED on the real IBM crate), and Windows' 260 includes the crate's own place and
    its terminating null: a host without long-path support has 259 - 77 - 1, 181 characters, for the directory the crate is in.
15. **Step 4 finds a committed file by digest among the crate's copies.** The real crate holds 31 copies of each logical circuit
    and 2 to 11 of each of its 23 QPY digests (the 17 a commitment names are held in 3 to 11 copies, the other six in 2 to 6; MEASURED,
    a test in `p1-crates` holds the figures), so altering only the copy a job ran is accepted by steps 1 to 8 while another copy keeps
    the digest. Step 9 refuses it: the manifest's digest of the copy that ran is no longer that file's (G1).
    `test_altering_only_the_copy_a_job_ran_...` holds it on the twin, where the morning and sheet-01 QPYs are one file.
16. **Nothing compares one job with another;** a Decode is held to the job of its known answer. A job copied byte for byte under new
    ids, with the manifest naming it and the read's records of it, is accepted by every step, as limit 1's fabricated run is: a test
    makes one with the real generator, which holds the records to one another and finds them agreeing, and the whole checker accepts
    a crate of five jobs where there were four.
17. **`qro:jobId` is REPORTED and compared with nothing.** A Job's times are the raw result's to say: where it carries no span they are
    compared with nothing.
18. **A number in a snapshot must be one binary64 holds exactly,** and every reader refuses an integer past 2^63 - 1 in magnitude.
19. **A crate that carries no term and no entity of P2's passes P2's steps,** whatever law Pubs and Compiles it holds; step 9 refuses it,
    wherever `qro.p2_measures` is present and the crate holds files under `question/` (its generator then writes P2's entities). A crate
    P2 has no law for holds none, its generator writes none, and step 9 accepts it with the rest (the seam section).
20. **The names, descriptions and groupings of the metadata** (the Datasets, the Compile's description, the question's text) are
    REPORTED, held by step 9 alone.
21. **Some classes of carried bytes are held by their file's digest and size alone.** The generator holds a field where it derives a
    value from it or holds it to another record; the rest is carried, and a changed leaf with only its file's digest resealed in the
    metadata is accepted (MEASURED by P1.2's first verifier: 391 of 2,283 leaf edits of the twin, 895 of 1,059 of the Atlas crate;
    by its second: 389 of 2,279 of the twin, 433 of the first 548 of the Atlas crate). The classes: the verdict files (every field);
    the device-run records' `law`, `code` and `source.fixed_at`; the metrics wrappers' fields but the job's id, the device,
    `job.created` and `metrics.timestamps.created` and `.running`, which G6 and G7 hold, and `metrics.timestamps.finished`, which G6
    holds only as the execution span's upper bound (a later one is accepted): `job`'s and `metrics`' other fields, `usage` among
    them, and `result_metadata`, a second copy of the execution span among them, which G6 does not compare with the raw result's; the
    submitting marker's `begun_at`; the read's README, logs and MANIFEST headers, and its MANIFEST entries for files the crate does not
    carry; Quantum-Film's licence, `LICENSE-quantum-film`, in both crates (every byte); in the real IBM crate, which the twin cannot
    show, the six prediction files (`prediction-backend.json` and `prediction-none.json` of each device, 11% of its bytes), each held
    by its digest and size alone, and in Quantum-Film's own five snapshots, which no MANIFEST lists, every value the claims and the
    values-digest leave out: every `date` but the newest, as the digest leaves every date out and the claims hold only the stamp and
    the newest date (the read's own snapshots are held by its MANIFEST's digests, G9) (MEASURED by P1.2's third verifier); and for
    Atlas the request's method, path and
    parameters but the circuit and the shots, and its response's other fields; the status file's fields but the job's id and its
    state; the score file's fields but the job's id (its own `circuit_sha256` and `commitment` among them); the result envelope's
    `path`, `taken_from`, `status` and `fetched_at`; each result's `observables` and `density_matrices`, the engine's numbers, which
    nothing reads; the counts of every setting but the all-Z one (eight of the nine of a real result), held by their sum alone, so
    that a shot moved between two outcomes of one of them is accepted; and a commitment line's `submitted_at`. A test holds a sample
    of each class as accepted: `tests/p1/test_p1_controls.py` on the twin, `tests/p1/test_p1_atlas_generate.py` on the Atlas crate,
    and `tests/p1crates/test_p1crates_controls.py` on the real IBM crate for the two classes only it holds.
22. **A failed build removes what it made and only that, so it can leave some of it.** The adapter lists each directory it makes,
    one level at a time, from the first that is missing down to `--out` and each that a file needs, and each file it writes, created
    anew, each with its identity (device and file number) as it was made, a file's from the handle that made it. A build that fails,
    at any point before it returns (what it reports among it), or is interrupted, unlinks each listed file that is still the regular
    file it wrote, reached through no listed directory that has changed, and then removes each listed directory, the deepest first,
    by `rmdir`, which removes only an empty one. Nothing is removed recursively, and nothing because its path was
    computed: the adapter holds no recursive delete. So what another puts in a directory the build made, during the build, stays, and
    so do that directory and every one above it; so does a file or a directory another puts in the place of one the build made, or
    a link in the place of a directory, through which nothing is removed at the time of the check; and so does what cannot be removed
    (a file another holds open). The line is the build's own verdict and says nothing of what was left. Each case is held in
    `tests/p1/test_p1_adapter.py` on a world built in the test's own temporary directory. **The checks are made at their time, not
    atomically.** Another party who can write in `--out` while the build runs, and so could write or remove its files itself, can swap
    a directory the build made for a link: the build's later writes then follow that link, as the checks guard the removals and not
    the writes; and in the window between a path's check and its unlink, a file behind a link put there in that window can be removed
    (MEASURED by P1.2's third verifier, with the swaps simulated). Closing that window needs handle-based calls, which Python's
    standard library lacks on Windows. The swap's other half: a link another party puts at the name of a directory the build has
    not yet made is followed, for the build finds a directory there and makes none; it writes its files through the link, into
    the link's target outside `--out`, and a build that fails removes those files, which it listed, and nothing that was there
    before (MEASURED by P1.2's fifth verifier, with the link put after the out was checked). Two more of the same kind: a
    directory's identity is taken by its path just after it is made, so a link swapped in between the two is listed in its
    place; and on a volume whose file numbers are all 0, the identity holds the device alone (BELIEVED; no such volume was tried).
23. **The out guard tells a place by its spelling once links are resolved and the extended-length prefix is dropped.** An
    `--out` spelt through a network share of the same disk (`\\localhost\C$\...`) names a place inside this repository or the
    checkout by a spelling `os.path.realpath` does not bring back to theirs, and so would pass the guard on a host where the share
    opens (BELIEVED, from the code). On this host the share is refused, "Network access is denied", so no build is made there
    (MEASURED by P1.2's fourth verifier).

## What a file costs a step

Steps 4, 5 and 8 keep of a file what they need of it and never its bytes, so a crate costs a step the parse of its largest file with
a few hundred bytes for each PUB and job beside it, not the sum of its files. **What a parse costs, MEASURED by P1.1 by shape**, as a
multiple of the file's size, the bytes read counted. No bound that a file cannot exceed is known; these are what was measured, not
limits: a shape not tried may cost more.

| a file of | made of | x the file |
|---|---|---|
| any JSON file the steps read (a job line, a record, a snapshot, a raw result's wrapper) | nested one-element arrays, depth 900, repeated | **46.0** |
| | nested single-key objects, depth 100 to 900 | 38.7 to 38.8 |
| | alternating array and single-key object, depth 400 | 40.8 |
| | flat: `[{},...]`, `[[0],...]`, `[{"a":0},...]` | 26.0 to 26.1 |
| | `[[],...]`; 10 MB of distinct two-character keys | 23.3; 25.2 |
| | `[1.5,...]`, `["abcdefgh",...]`, `[0,...]`, `[null,...]` | 10.1, 7.2, 6.3, 3.7 |
| a valid snapshot (step 8: the digest spells each number again, as an object) | one `general_qlists` entry whose `qubits` are n zeros | **147.3** |
| | one gate whose `qubits` are n zeros | 144.9 |
| | `general_qlists` entries of `qubits: [0]`; of no qubits | 35.0; 22.9 |
| | `qubits` rows of one entry; entries of `general`; gates of one parameter | 15.6; 13.3; 14.3 |
| an IBM result's array | data that do not deflate, at the reader's cap (67,108,608 bytes inflated, from an 89,506,487-byte file) | **447.5 MB**: 6.67 x the inflated size |
| | data that deflate, at the cap, from 88,003 bytes | 134.6 MB: 2.01 x the inflated size |
| a known-answer circuit (refused after it is read) | lines of two characters, all alike or distinct | **21.7** |
| | blank lines; three characters; one character; eight | 18.7; 17.1; 10.3; 9.4 |

The real crate's largest file is a snapshot of 1.1 MB, which costs step 8 about 160 MB at the worst shape and a few megabytes as it
is. Nothing bounds a file's size but the crate's, so a host fails (exit 3) when it cannot parse the crate's largest file; step 3,
which is P0's, reads every raw result before these steps run, and what it gives on a MemoryError is P0's (FORMAT.md item 16).

**Step 5's time on a result at the reader's cap.** A crate holding one IBM result at the reader's cap (33,554,240 rows, 67,108,480
bytes inflated, from an 88,003-byte file) takes step 5 **6.1 s** (MEASURED here), against 0.13 s for step 4 and 0.15 s for step 8:
step 5 tallies the rows once for the known answer's verdict and again for each PUB. It is linear in the results.

## The tests and the stages

- **`p1`** (`tests/p1`, in the quick budget): P1.1's checker-side tests and the producer's: the readers on the real twin files and
  every kind of fault (`test_p1_records.py`), the generator on the twin and its 114 planted faults (`test_p1_generate.py`), Atlas's
  (`test_p1_atlas_generate.py`), the seam with a stand-in (`test_p1_seam.py`, with `p1_standin.py` to run the adapter as a process with a stand-in that raises) and with P2's own module (`test_p1_merged.py`), the adapter as a process and in process (`test_p1_adapter.py`, with `p1_hosts.py` and `p1_pinned.py` to run its command line under
  a host a test simulates and on a toy checkout), the
  seven controls and the toolkit (`test_p1_controls.py`), the steps swept over the twin (`test_p1_sweeps.py`) and the generators'
  registration (`test_p1_generators.py`). The helpers (`p1_support.py`) memoise what is parsed from identical bytes and share one
  hard-linked working copy of the twin, restored after each test.
- **`p1-crates`** (`tests/p1crates`, out of the quick budget, skipped by name without `QRO_MOTH_ROOT`): both real crates built by the
  adapter as a process and checked by the command line as one; the adapter's rule on the real checkout (the 930 files the IBM
  crate takes from it and the 30 of the Atlas crate are each HEAD's own, by git's hash of a blob); the design's measurements on the real bytes (68 of 68 commitments
  held to IBM's echo, 12 values-digests in six classes, the link rule's 57 / 8 / 1, 31 of 31 known answers, the decoded counts of
  all 68 records, the six Atlas law results read whole with their 1,995 to 2,027 layouts and the vector at |z| 2.15 against 172.96);
  the seven controls on hard-linked copies of the real IBM crate, the source checked unchanged; and the steps swept over every
  entity of both crates.
- The controls also run in `control-tests` and `controls-run`, on the twin, with no moth-quantum.
